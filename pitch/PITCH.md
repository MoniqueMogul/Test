## 1 · Two-Minute Verbal Pitch Script

*(~290 words · ~2:00 at a natural speaking pace · beat markers in brackets)*

**[0:00 — the hook]**
Let me ask you something. When was the last time you shipped a spec that an engineer actually read end to end? Most PMs live in one of two failure modes: we over-spec without ever building anything — or we "vibe-code" with AI and end up with a pile of unstructured slop. There's a better way, and it takes exactly four steps.

**[0:25 — the framework]**
Step one: **Brainstorm to PRD.** You don't write the PRD — you make the AI interview *you*, one clarifying question at a time, until every assumption is on the table. Step two: **Design exploration.** Before you commit to a single pixel, you get three clearly differentiated design directions and pick with your eyes, not your imagination. Step three: **Implementation spec.** The AI writes the architecture — performance, scalability, safety, data models — and you read it, challenge it, and learn from it. Step four: **Implementation.** It builds the MVP, you test it, and you push it to GitHub like a real engineering team.

**[1:10 — the proof]**
In our live demo we built BabyBedtimeTeller — an app that writes and *narrates* a personalized, calming bedtime story for your kid in seconds. Real product, real OpenAI calls, real deployed HTML. Thirty minutes, start to finish.

**[1:30 — the payoff]**
You walk away with three things: a working prototype with professional architecture, an AI engineer that's been coached on *your* patterns and preferences — and something rarer: you actually understand the tradeoffs and data flow of the thing you built. That's technical growth you can take into your next roadmap meeting.

**[1:50 — the close]**
All you need is a GitHub account, Claude Code, and an API key. Stop specifying. Start building. Let's go.

## 2 · Summary for a PM Audience

**What it is.** A 90-minute hands-on workshop (AI PM Bootcamp, Session 1) teaching a repeatable
4-step "vibe coding" framework that takes a product idea from brainstorm to a working, AI-native
prototype — built by you, coached by you, without an engineering team.

**The problem it solves.** PMs today split into two camps: those who over-spec and never build,
and those who prompt an AI aimlessly and get unmaintainable demos. Neither produces artifacts an
org can act on. The framework adds just enough structure to make AI-built prototypes *credible*:
a real PRD, deliberate design selection, a reviewed architecture, and version-controlled code.

**The method — four prompts, four artifacts:**

| Step | You do | You get |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Brainstorm → PRD | Answer the AI's clarifying questions (one at a time) | A complete PRD: problem, personas & journeys, MVP scope, success metrics, corner cases |
| 2 · Design exploration | Review 3 deliberately differentiated landing-page styles | A chosen design direction, picked from real options |
| 3 · Implementation spec | Challenge the AI's architecture doc | A plan covering performance, scalability, safety, data models, observability |
| 4 · Implementation | Test the build, report bugs, iterate | A functional MVP with live AI API calls, pushed to your GitHub |

**Demo case.** BabyBedtimeTeller: parents enter a child's name and theme; the app generates a
short, calming, age-appropriate story and narrates it with soothing TTS — story in under 10
seconds, guardrails for young kids, replay support, all running locally against the OpenAI API.

**Why it matters for your practice.** The deliverable isn't just the prototype. It's (1) an AI
pair-engineer trained on your preferences and anti-patterns, and (2) genuine technical literacy —
you can now discuss architecture, tradeoffs, and data flow from firsthand experience rather than
secondhand summary. Optional extensions: AI-generated architecture diagrams ("reverse
mentorship"), turning your codebase into a course, and auto-generating a portfolio.

**Prerequisites.** GitHub account · Claude Code access · an LLM API key (OpenAI or other) ·
VS Code or Cursor with Node.js and Python.
