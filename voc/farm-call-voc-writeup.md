## 2 · Questions the consumer (Monique) asked — and the answers she received

These are the consumer's own questions — the strongest signal of what she needs and doesn't yet know.

### Q1 — On city land-use agreements: "Is that just like going to City Hall… or is that still USDA?" (@11:56–12:08)
**Kesha's answer:** City/county, not USDA — "it's parks, so city county" (@12:11). Pick a receptive, less-saturated area. Her own example: North Little Rock lets her nonprofit use **10 acres of Parks & Rec land at no cost** under a land-use agreement to grow food and run farm tours (@10:45–11:50) — though on land-use ground you "can't get a farm number, we can't get funding for it" (@11:45). She grows greens there "for food insecure persons in the city to pick" (@13:27).

### Q2 — On tour logistics with a transitional-housing property: does hosting tours disrupt residents? (@26:35)
**Kesha's answer (assembled from @32:28–34:36, @44:30–45:18):** The tour model works "on any farm," including self-guided setups. Structure the land so "the front makes money and the back is private" (@44:30). Related tactics she offered: free aluminum signage via the Department of Transportation/Highway Public Safety by leveraging city relationships (@32:43–33:34); self-guided touches like "plant a seed on your way out" or "leave a note for a loved one in the soil" (@33:44–34:00); school tours — "the school buses, they brought the school bus to my house" (@50:07); college ag students as hosts, potentially stipended from city funds (@34:10–34:32); U-pick days where harvesters keep a share — "so you got free labor at your farm" (@45:02); ~**$20/person** pricing including a bottle of water and a snack, booked and prepaid online, **no refunds, transferable only** (@45:18–46:55).

### Q3 — On livestock: "What would I have to go through with that?" (@26:35)
**Kesha's answer:** Easier animals are **chickens and ducks** — "make sure your chickens are vaccinated" (@29:31, in the context of her own unvaccinated hen's Marek's disease **[transcribed "Merrick's"]**, @3:13). Ducks are protective; aggression depends on numbers and type (@29:33). Animal behavior calms when food is secure: "the more food they have… the less competitive they are" (@29:50). Also suggested a **stocked fish pond with a small bridge** for visitor feeding — a memorable, low-cost attraction (@30:21–30:34). Livestock summary confirmed on-call: chickens, ducks, fish (@44:05–44:23).

### Q4 — On repeat tour visitors: "How would you say to navigate if they have already been there before?… do we suggest something else?" (@46:55)
**Kesha's answer:** Not necessarily a second location — "always be improving the farm… just like the zoo, they come up with creative things to keep you coming back," e.g., "this year we planted 100 rosemary bushes and it's a sensory walk," possibly with music (@47:16–47:53).

### Q5 — On property readiness: "What would be the plan for me to get ready?" (@51:36)
Context she supplied: two properties of her own plus her mom's Palm Beach property (already growing **mangoes, lemons, beans**); the Tampa house has **two fruit trees** and volunteer **aloe**; she has already submitted farm-number applications with property appraisals (@51:36, @56:42).
**Kesha's answer:** Prepare to apply for **EQIP at every property**; get a **farm number for every property**; audit each parcel for crop-producing trees (e.g., elderberry); **NRCS will want to see something in the ground**, even imperfect seeding (@52:05–52:25). She'll send a written plan including **conservation practices** to prepare for — specifically a **high tunnel**, irrigation, composting — and advises negotiating a **five-year in-kind lease** on any city land because conservation funding is in play (@50:32–51:19). She needs lot sizes to finalize; Monique will send property appraisals with drawings (@56:17–56:51).

### Q6 — On the planned local event: "Are we doing a class after the YouTube interview? What are we doing after?" (@52:51)
**Kesha's answer:** An event where attendees "pitch in to buy some food"; she'll send info on the September conference; she wants to come promote it and "maybe I can sell some books"; venue suggestion: **a church** ("they have a van, they have a kitchen, and they have an auditorium") (@53:22–54:20). Target timing: **end of July / very beginning of August** (@55:31). Monique will find the venue and produce a flyer; a college-instructor friend will mobilize students for attendance/social media (@55:37–56:06).

## 3 · Unprompted consumer statements (context, pain points, and workarounds in her own words)

These weren't answers to questions, but the consumer volunteered them — each is verbatim or near-verbatim with a timestamp.

1. **Tool frustration — calendar/data loss (@4:57):** Google Calendar entries "are disappearing, like I have to keep refreshing it for it to pop back up." Sellers vet callers by name and stall: "it's three weeks" for a callback. She's considering having intermediaries "have some of the conversations on my behalf," especially with cattle/horse-farm owners: "they're gonna be the ones that shoot first… it says no trespassing."
4. **Transitional-housing vision and target market (@19:21–20:52):** Homes for veterans ("we want to serve those who have served us") and people with intellectual disabilities; not currently targeting people with substance-abuse histories or those on the sex-offender registry ("I know they need housing. However…"). One home is planned with a farm in back — "I think that would be like a healing thing for them… doing something in a nice garden."
5. **Local political opening (@21:29–22:24):** Met the **City of Winter Haven commissioner** at "Coffee with the Commissioner"; he said "our homeless population is growing, food insecurity is growing. How can we bring it here?" and offered staff contacts. He may leave office soon (candidate meetings in coming weeks), so she feels time pressure to discuss land before he goes. Her read: "he seems very eager to put his stamp on it."
6. **Prior program experience she can build on (@48:16–49:40):** Helped run Tampa's "Soul Walk" — a guided bus tour of Black historical sites with aluminum plaques; partnered with a museum to hang underserved kids' artwork and got entry fees waived for students receiving **SNAP benefits** (parents sign a form), plus lunch stipends. "That does give me some ideas to see how I can try to tap back in."
7. **Pet insurance failure (@43:24):** After her dog's stroke, insurance "didn't cover anything… I'm like, so I just been paying you for years for you to stay not covered." A $6,000 MRI was quoted for a nine-pound dog. Her workaround advice: "ask them ahead of time, like, what does it really cover… because any little thing they like to say, oh no, that's not covered."
8. **Squeamishness boundary for hands-on livestock work (@27:58):** The Extension cattle-birthing training ("sometimes you have to reach in there") was a hard no: "y'all take this bag back… it was too much." She'd hire help for that layer of farm work.

## 6 · Interpretation (clearly labeled — the only non-transcript content in this document)

*Relevance of this VOC to FarmQuest, offered as analysis:* the consumer independently validates three of FarmQuest's foundational assumptions — (1) **farm tours are a viable revenue/engagement model** that even small operators want turnkey help with (her top question was tour logistics); (2) **school and community partnerships are the distribution channel** (school buses to farms, SNAP-waived museum entry, church vans, college students as hosts all appear as working patterns); and (3) **food insecurity is the framing that unlocks municipal support** (the commissioner's own words: "food insecurity is growing. How can we bring it here?"). The transcript also surfaces a persona FarmQuest's PRD doesn't yet serve: the **new/aspiring farm operator** who needs the tour experience productized for her, not just for schools visiting established farms.
