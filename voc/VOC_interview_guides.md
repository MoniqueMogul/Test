# FarmQuest — Voice of Customer (VOC) Interview Guides

**Product:** FarmQuest — a choose-your-own-adventure learning game (grades 6–12) where the story starts in class, unlocks via QR codes at a real local farm, and resolves back in class. Main pain point addressed: food insecurity is taught as a statistic, not an experience.

**Purpose of this document:** ready-to-run interview scripts for FarmQuest's four customer types — **students, teachers, farmers, and city officials** — in interview style, with blank answer fields so this same file becomes the write-up record for each conversation.

**Ground rules for every interview (no-fabrication protocol):**
1. Record (with permission) or take verbatim notes; write answers in the customer's own words wherever possible.
2. Never fill an answer field the customer didn't actually provide — leave it blank and mark **[not asked]** or **[declined]**.
3. Ask about their *current* behavior before showing the concept; don't pitch first.
4. One question at a time; silence is fine; "tell me more" beats a new question.
5. Log the interview metadata below for every session.

## Guide 1 · Students (grades 6–12)

**Objective:** learn how students actually experience field trips and food-systems lessons, what "boring vs. cool" means to them, and whether a story-driven farm quest would genuinely engage them.

**Recruiting:** 5–8 students across grades 6–12, mixed gender, at least 2 who have been on a farm/agriculture field trip. Recruit via a partner teacher.

**⚠️ Minors protocol:** obtain parent/guardian consent AND student assent; a teacher or guardian present; no personal questions about the student's own food security at home — the topic is explored through fiction and community framing only; no last names in notes.

**Warm-up**
- S0. What grade are you in, and what's your favorite class or game right now?
  > **Answer:**

**Section A — Current experience (before showing the concept)**
- S1. Tell me about the last field trip you went on. What do you actually remember from it?
  > **Answer:**
- S2. What was the most boring part? What (if anything) was the best part?
  > **Answer:**
- S3. When a teacher hands out a worksheet during a trip or after it, what do you honestly do with it?
  > **Answer:**
- S4. Have you ever learned about where food comes from, or why some neighborhoods don't have grocery stores? What do you remember about how that was taught?
  > **Answer:**
- S5. When you and your friends are given phones/Chromebooks for a school activity, what makes you actually pay attention vs. switch to something else?
  > **Answer:**
- S6. What's a game or app you genuinely like right now? What makes it not feel like "school stuff"?
  > **Answer:**

**Section B — Concept reaction (show the FarmQuest one-liner / landing page now)**
- S7. In your own words, what do you think this is?
  > **Answer:**
- S8. Imagine you're at a farm and you scan a QR code at the barn — the next chapter of a mystery opens, and the clue is on a real feed chart on the wall. Honest reaction: cool or cringe? Why?
  > **Answer:**
- S9. You'd play in a group of 2–4 sharing one phone. Better or worse than playing alone? Why?
  > **Answer:**
- S10. The story's choices change the ending — your group's ending might be different from another group's. Does that make you want to compare endings? Would you replay it?
  > **Answer:**
- S11. One story theme is about a neighborhood losing its grocery store and what the characters do about it. Does a story like that interest you? What would make it feel real instead of preachy?

## Guide 2 · Teachers (grades 6–12; agriscience, science, FCS, social studies)

**Objective:** understand trip-prep workload, curriculum-connection pain, AI-content trust requirements, and what would make a teacher adopt, review, and re-run FarmQuest.

**Recruiting:** 4–6 teachers who have run at least one field trip in the past 2 years; include at least one agriscience/CTE teacher and one who teaches food systems or nutrition content.

**Warm-up**
- T0. What do you teach, which grades, and how many field trips do you run in a typical year?
  > **Answer:**

**Section A — Current behavior & pain**
- T1. Walk me through your last field trip, start to finish: how did you prepare, what happened on the day, what happened after?
  > **Answer:**
- T2. How many hours did prep take, and which part was the most annoying?
  > **Answer:**
- T3. What materials (if any) did the destination provide? Were they usable as-is?
  > **Answer:**
- T4. How do you currently connect a trip to your curriculum or standards? How well does that work?
  > **Answer:**
- T5. How do you teach food insecurity / food access today, if at all? What materials exist, and what's wrong with them?
  > **Answer:**
- T6. What's your policy (and your school's) on students using phones during a trip?
  > **Answer:**
- T7. Have you used any AI-generated content with students? What would you need to see before trusting it?
  > **Answer:**

**Section B — Concept reaction (show concept + teacher-approval flow now)**
- T8. First reaction — what problem does this solve for you, if any?
  > **Answer:**
- T9. Every story is AI-drafted, machine-moderated, then **you review every branch and approve before students can play**. Is that reassuring or is that a new workload problem? How long would you tolerate review taking?
  > **Answer:**
- T10. The flagship story theme is food insecurity, handled with dignity (characters solve problems, nobody is a case study). What are your guardrails for teaching this topic — what would make you regenerate or reject a section?
  > **Answer:**
- T11. Students join with a group code — no accounts, no student data collected. Does that change how easily you could get this approved by your school/district?
  > **Answer:**
- T12. On trip day you'd get a live view of which groups checked in at which stations. Useful or noise?
  > **Answer:**
- T13. After the trip: a reflection chapter plus a CSV of choices/reflections. How would you actually use that — grading, discussion, evidence for admin?
  > **Answer:**
- T14. What would make this a "no" for you regardless of quality? (Probe: tech reliability, admin approval, parent pushback, time.)
  > **Answer:**

**Section C — Adoption & value**
- T15. If this were free this year (pilot), what would it take for you to run it with one class?

## Guide 3 · Farmers / Farm Operators (agritourism hosts, current or aspiring)

**Objective:** understand what hosting school groups costs and earns them today, what would make station QR signs + story tours worth it, and what support a small or new operator needs.

**Recruiting:** 3–5 operators: at least one who already hosts school tours, one agritourism-curious operator who doesn't yet, and one new/aspiring operator (e.g., nonprofit or urban farm). *(Note: one aspiring-operator interview already exists — see `farm-call-voc-writeup.md`; use it, don't re-invent it.)*

**Warm-up**
- F0. Tell me about your operation — what you grow/raise, acreage, and who works it.
  > **Answer:**

**Section A — Current behavior & pain**
- F1. Do school groups or the public visit your farm today? Walk me through what a visit actually looks like.
  > **Answer:**
- F2. What does hosting a group cost you — time, staff, prep, liability, wear and tear?
  > **Answer:**
- F3. What do you get out of it — revenue, sales, goodwill, repeat bookings? Is it worth it today?
  > **Answer:**
- F4. What do visitors and students not understand about your farm that you wish they did?
  > **Answer:**
- F5. Does your farm play any role in local food access — donations, food-bank partnerships, U-pick, SNAP/market programs? Is that story ever told to visitors?
  > **Answer:**
- F6. What's your connectivity like across the property (cell signal / Wi-Fi)?
  > **Answer:**
- F7. (If not yet hosting) What has stopped you from doing tours so far? What would you need productized for you?
  > **Answer:**

**Section B — Concept reaction (show concept + farm setup flow now)**
- F8. Setup would be: one 30-minute onboarding call about your stations and stories, then we send weatherproof QR signs you post once. Gut reaction — realistic or hassle?
  > **Answer:**
- F9. Students would move station to station following the story, in groups, with a teacher managing them. What worries you — safety, biosecurity, animals, crops, liability?
  > **Answer:**
- F10. The stories weave in facts you provide about your farm. What 3 things would you want every student to walk away knowing?
  > **Answer:**
- F11. Would being a "FarmQuest destination" plausibly bring you more school bookings or repeat visits? What proof would you need?
  > **Answer:**
- F12. At season's end you'd get a report: students hosted, engagement highlights, student quotes mentioning your farm. Valuable or fluff?
  > **Answer:**
- F13. If schools paid for the software and you hosted for your normal fee, is there any world where you'd also pay — for listing, marketing, or extra content?

## Guide 4 · City Officials (commissioners, Parks & Rec, community development, county extension)

**Objective:** understand how food insecurity ranks on their agenda, what programs they can fund or host on public land, and what would make them sponsor or champion FarmQuest-style programming.

**Recruiting:** 2–4 officials/staff: a city commissioner or mayor's office staffer, a Parks & Rec director, a community development / CDBG administrator, and/or a county extension agent. *(Note from prior VOC: a Winter Haven commissioner independently said "food insecurity is growing. How can we bring it here?" — that framing opens doors; cite local data, not our product, first.)*

**Warm-up**
- C0. Tell me about your role and what you're responsible for in the city/county.
  > **Answer:**

**Section A — Current agenda & pain**
- C1. Where does food insecurity sit on your council's/department's priority list right now? What's driving that?
  > **Answer:**
- C2. What food-access or youth programs does the city currently run or fund? What's working and what isn't?
  > **Answer:**
- C3. How do you currently connect schools with community assets (parks, farms, markets)? Who owns that relationship?
  > **Answer:**
- C4. Is there public land — parks, easements, unused parcels — that has been (or could be) used for growing food or agricultural education? What's the process to use it?
  > **Answer:**
- C5. When a nonprofit or small program approaches you, what makes you take it seriously vs. politely stall?
  > **Answer:**
- C6. What funding mechanisms do you control or influence — CDBG, discretionary funds, Parks & Rec budget, state pass-through? What are their calendars?
  > **Answer:**

**Section B — Concept reaction (show concept + a school/farm pilot summary now)**
- C7. First reaction — where does something like this fit in your city's goals (education, food access, agritourism, youth engagement, economic development)?
  > **Answer:**
- C8. If local schools and a local farm ran this pilot, what would the city want out of it — metrics, visibility, resident stories? What would you need to see to call it a success?
  > **Answer:**
- C9. Could city-owned land or a Parks & Rec site host stations (a garden, a market, a demonstration plot) as a FarmQuest destination for schools without farm access? What would that take?
  > **Answer:**
- C10. Who else in the city/county would need to say yes — and who might say no, and why?
  > **Answer:**
- C11. Is there a funding path here — sponsoring school participation, funding a public-land site, CDBG eligibility — and what would the application need to show?
  > **Answer:**
- C12. Election cycles matter: what's your window for championing something new, and what makes a program politically safe to put your name on?
  > **Answer:**

**Closing**
- C13. Would you take a 15-minute briefing after our pilot's first trip, and who should be in that room?
